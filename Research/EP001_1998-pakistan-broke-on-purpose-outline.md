# Documentary Outline — EP001 — 1998: The Year Pakistan Went Broke on Purpose

**Recommended structure type:** Custom — "Countdown to Crisis" (Historical base, sharpened for urgency)
**Reasoning:** This isn't a panoramic historical piece and it isn't a policy explainer — it's a single deliberate decision (testing a nuclear weapon) that set off a fast, escalating financial emergency with an actual ticking clock (reserves draining toward zero over roughly six months). A loose Beginning → Turning Points → Consequences → Today shape would undersell that urgency. Instead, this uses a tightened cause-and-effect countdown: one choice → immediate consequence → the emergency measure → how close it actually got → the rescue → what it left behind. This is EP001, so there's no prior-episode pattern to react to — but starting the channel on a countdown structure (rather than the more generic historical shape) sets an early precedent for varying pace and shape by topic, not just by category.
**Estimated length:** 14–18 minutes (rich, well-sourced timeline and multiple numeric beats justify a longer runtime than a single-issue explainer, but it shouldn't stretch into a sprawling general history of the nuclear program)

## Structure

1. **The Test**
   - Core content: May 28 & 30, 1998 — Pakistan's Chagai-I and Chagai-II tests, in direct response to India's Pokhran-II days earlier. Brief, not the focus — this is context, not a nuclear-program documentary.
   - Purpose in the arc: Establishes the single deliberate decision everything else in the episode traces back to.

2. **The Bill Comes Due**
   - Core content: Within days, US sanctions under the Glenn Amendment and Japan's aid suspension hit. The pre-test IMF program (Oct 1997, positively reviewed in early 1998) was already fragile — this is the moment it breaks.
   - Purpose in the arc: Shows the fallout was fast and foreseeable, not a slow-burn surprise — sets up "on purpose" honestly (the test was the deliberate act; the economic consequence was the foreseeable price).

3. **The Freeze**
   - Core content: The government freezes up to $11 billion in foreign currency accounts to stop a bank run. Finance Minister Sartaj Aziz's public justification. What this meant for ordinary account holders who'd been encouraged to bank dollars inside Pakistan.
   - Purpose in the arc: The emotional core — a government reaching into citizens' own savings. This is where the episode's human stakes live.

4. **The Countdown**
   - Core content: Reserves sliding to ~$400 million by late November 1998 — translate that into something visceral (weeks of import cover, not just a raw number). The dual exchange rate gap (46.0 official vs. 54.2 parallel) as a symptom of how strained the system was.
   - Purpose in the arc: Makes "how close to collapse" concrete instead of abstract — this is the tension peak.

5. **The Rescue**
   - Core content: January 14, 1999 — IMF approves the second ESAF arrangement, ~$575 million disbursed. Paris and London Club debt rescheduling follows. Pakistan avoids formal default.
   - Purpose in the arc: Resolution — but a costly one (more debt, more conditions), not a clean win.

6. **What It Left Behind**
   - Core content: Lasting distrust in keeping dollar savings inside Pakistani banks. Connect to the pattern of repeated IMF programs (without over-promising the next episode — a light thread, not a hard sequel tease).
   - Purpose in the arc: Answers "why does this matter to me, today" — closes the spine.

## Spine

One deliberate decision — testing a nuclear weapon — pushed Pakistan's economy to the edge of collapse within six months, and the way the government protected itself (freezing citizens' own savings) still shapes how Pakistanis think about banking their money today.

## Hook (first 30 seconds)

Open on the freeze itself, not the tests: state plainly that one week in 1998, the government simply stopped Pakistanis from withdrawing their own dollars — up to $11 billion, gone from reach overnight — then ask why a government would do that to its own citizens. Deliberately withhold *why* until Section 1–2 delivers it. (Note: a real named account-holder's story would make this hook far stronger — flagged as an open research gap; do not invent one. If found later, restructure the hook around that person specifically.)

## Ending

Leave the viewer with the practical, present-tense point: this is a real reason many older Pakistanis still keep dollars outside the formal banking system rather than trusting an account — and note, without turning it into a cliffhanger, that this wouldn't be the last time Pakistan came this close to running out of money.

- Do not use the GDP growth figures from the research brief — flagged Tier 4/unverified.
