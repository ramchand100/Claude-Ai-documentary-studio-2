# Documentary Outline — EP004 — The NFC Award: The Formula That Divides Pakistan's Money

**Recommended structure type:** Custom — "Institutional Arc" (Historical base, chronological spine, the institution itself as protagonist rather than any province)
**Reasoning:** Chosen over a comparative/parallel-provincial structure specifically for neutrality safety — a chronological shape can't accidentally give one province more narrative weight than another, because no province drives the story; the timeline does. This also means the episode doesn't depend on having every province's position equally documented (Punjab's isn't, as of this research), since provincial viewpoints appear as supporting detail within the chronology rather than as structural pillars. Echoes EP001's "Countdown to Crisis" shape, which worked.

**Estimated total runtime:** 12-16 minutes as a starting range — per the lesson from EP001 and EP002, this is a planning guide only; the script-writing skill's own runtime self-check (word count ÷ 140wpm vs. this range) will confirm or correct it once drafted.

## Structure

1. **The Formula Nobody's Heard Of**
   - Core content: Establish what the NFC Award actually is and why it matters before naming it — federal tax revenue gets split between the centre and the provinces, and among the provinces, by a formula almost nobody outside government knows exists.
   - Purpose in the arc: The hook and the spine question in one — introduces the stakes (this decides how much money your province actually has) before the mechanism.

2. **One Number, One Formula (1973-2009)**
   - Core content: Article 160 establishes the NFC. For over three decades, population alone decided each province's share — a formula that structurally favored the most populous province and left Sindh, KP, and Balochistan wanting change. The 2001 and 2006 commissions both failed to reach any agreement at all.
   - Purpose in the arc: Sets up the long-standing grievance that the 7th Award would later address — establishes why 2009 was a breakthrough, not just another award.

3. **The Breakthrough (2009)**
   - Core content: The 7th NFC Award moves beyond population-only distribution — a multi-criteria formula answering specific, sourced demands: Sindh's push for revenue collection to count, KP's push for poverty/backwardness, Balochistan's push for land area. The provinces' overall share of the divisible pool also rises. The 18th Amendment's non-reduction guarantee (Article 160(3A)) locks in future protection.
   - Purpose in the arc: The high point — a genuine negotiated compromise that worked, which makes the next section's freeze land harder by contrast.

4. **The Freeze (2015-2025)**
   - Core content: The 7th Award's five-year term expires in 2015. No successor is agreed for a decade — the formula keeps applying by default. A full decade of provincial finances running on an award nobody re-negotiated.
   - Purpose in the arc: The tension/stakes section — ten years is a long time for underlying realities (population shifts, economic changes, new fiscal burdens like the FATA merger) to outpace a frozen formula.

5. **The Attempt (2025-2026)**
   - Core content: The 11th NFC is finally constituted in August 2025. Trace its real, sourced stumbles: a postponed inaugural session, a first meeting that didn't happen until December, eight working groups where six never met, both sides publicly blaming each other for the delay. Include the federal Planning Minister's own public call for reform — not just a provinces-vs-centre framing.
   - Purpose in the arc: Brings the story to the present tense — this is happening (or not happening) right now, which is the episode's real hook for currency.

6. **What's Actually At Stake**
   - Core content: Land on why this isn't just bureaucratic process — it's why one province might have more for schools and roads than another, structurally, regardless of need. Close on the structural critique from research literature (a population-heavy formula's incentive effects) as a technical point, not a partisan one.
   - Purpose in the arc: Closes the spine with the viewer's actual stake in an otherwise abstract institutional story.

## Spine

A single formula, unchanged in any meaningful way since 2009, decides how much money each Pakistani province actually has — and the commission meant to finally update it has spent a year struggling to even hold its meetings.

## Hook (first 30 seconds)

Open on the concrete, felt consequence before naming the institution: something like "the province you live in has already decided, by a formula you've never seen, how much money it has for your local school or hospital this year" — a number/stakes-first hook, not a definition of "NFC Award." Never open with "The National Finance Commission is..."

## Ending

Land on the viewer's actual stake — not a summary of the timeline, but why an abstract formula decides something concrete about their own province's ability to fund things they actually use. Avoid resolving the stall artificially; the honest ending is that this is unresolved, same as the auto policy episode's honest ending.

## Notes carried forward to Script Writing

- **Do not state Punjab's position on the formula.** It wasn't sourced. Don't imply its absence means Punjab has no view, or silently assume its position from the incentive structure — just don't address it, or note factually that its explicit position wasn't part of this research if the gap needs acknowledging on screen.
- **Balochistan's specific proposed numbers (82%→57%, 2.7%→15%) are flagged as needing direct-source confirmation** — hedge with attribution language ("reportedly proposed") unless confirmed before scripting, per the research brief.
- **Phrase the 11th NFC's current stall with a date-stamp** ("as of [date]...") — this is a live, moving story, same handling as EP002's AIDEP 2026-31 status.
- **Represent Sindh, KP, and Balochistan's positions in roughly even weight and with equal factual grounding** — each gets its actual stated reasoning, not a strawman, per the universal neutrality rule and the sensitivity flag on this topic.
- **The population-incentive critique (Section 6) is a structural/academic point (PIDE research), not attributed to any province or party** — keep it framed as a technical critique of the mechanism, not a talking point for one side.
