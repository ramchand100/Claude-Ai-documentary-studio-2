# Documentary Outline — EP002 — The Auto Tax: Why a Small Car Costs a House Deposit

**Recommended structure type:** Custom — "The Price Tag Investigation" (Economic Issue base, adapted for a live/unresolved policy fight)
**Reasoning:** This isn't a settled-history piece like EP001, and a plain "Policy" template (Why introduced → How it works → Winners → Losers → Future) undersells two things that make this story distinctive: (1) it starts from a genuinely everyday consumer pain point — a specific, relatable price shock — before it becomes a policy explainer, and (2) the "Future" section can't responsibly promise resolution, because there isn't one yet — the successor policy is being fought over inside government as this brief is being written. So this borrows the Economic Issue shape (everyday problem → why it happens → data → stakeholders → impact) but replaces a tidy "solutions" ending with an honest "the fight over the answer is happening right now, and here's what to watch for" close — matching what the research actually found, not what a template expects to find.

**Estimated total runtime:** 15-20 minutes as a starting range — treat this loosely. EP001's outline estimate (14-18 min) ended up overstating the drafted script by several minutes once actual word counts came in; don't lock this number until a draft exists. This topic has at least as much sourced material as EP001 (arguably more, given the CCP report's depth), so a longer runtime is defensible if the content supports it — but size sections to what they need, not to hit this number.

## Structure

1. **The Price Tag**
   - Core content: Open on the concrete shock — the same small car costing multiples more in Pakistan than in a neighboring market (the Suzuki Alto comparison). Frame it in terms an ordinary viewer feels immediately — the title's own "house deposit" framing.
   - Purpose in the arc: The hook and the spine question in one: why does this specific, ordinary object cost this much here?

2. **The Tax Stack**
   - Core content: Break down what's actually inside that price — customs duty, regulatory duty, sales tax (raised to 25% on locally-assembled vehicles in March 2023), federal excise duty. Use the real regulatory instrument (SRO 1152(I)/2025) as a concrete anchor rather than an abstract "there are taxes."
   - Purpose in the arc: Answers "how" before "why" — makes the price tag legible as a stack of specific decisions, not a mysterious markup.

3. **Why It Was Built This Way**
   - Core content: The history — Deletion Program (1987-2004, real indigenization gains: 56-70% cars, 81-88% motorcycles, but capped at low-value parts), Tariff-Based System (2006-16, CBU tariffs 50%-100%+), AIDP/AIDEP (2016-26). The protectionist logic: build a domestic industry or be flooded by imports.
   - Purpose in the arc: Earns the policy some good faith before complicating it — this wasn't arbitrary, it had a stated goal.

4. **Did It Work?**
   - Core content: The evaluation, using the CCP's own findings — market still "highly concentrated" (HHI ≈2,765, above the regulator's own threshold) even as new entrants (Kia, Hyundai, Sazgar) chip into the Big Three's ≈79-80% share. Ground this in two concrete cases: the 2013 CCP action against Toyota Pakistan for abusing its market position, and the 2015 cartelization penalty. This is where the episode gets critical, not just explanatory.
   - Purpose in the arc: The turn — protection built something, but what it built also had room to behave badly, and the regulator's own numbers say it's not "mission accomplished."

5. **The Fight Happening Right Now**
   - Core content: AIDEP 2021-26 expired 30 June 2026; the successor is caught between the Ministry of Commerce (faster cuts, CBU tariffs toward 15%) and the Ministry of Industries and Production (slower, 40-75% by engine size); Ishaq Dar's committee reached only "in-principle" consensus as of 13 August 2026. This is live and could have moved by the time this airs — script should be phrased to age reasonably even if the exact status shifts (e.g. "as of [date], still not finalized" rather than a flat present-tense claim).
   - Purpose in the arc: The tension peak — genuinely open-ended, not resolved for the viewer artificially.

6. **What It Means For You**
   - Core content: Land on the buyer's stake, not a policy wrap-up. Use the sourced thesis line — tariff cuts don't automatically become lower prices unless something forces assemblers to pass savings on ("tariff cuts must reach the buyer, not disappear into OEM margins"). End on what a viewer should actually watch for (does the sticker price move, or just the import duty on paper) rather than a false "problem solved" note.
   - Purpose in the arc: Closes the spine with agency for the viewer — not just information, but what to look for next.

## Spine

Decades of tariff protection built Pakistan's car industry from nothing — but the same protection that grew it also let it get away with a highly concentrated, occasionally abusive market, and the fight over how much of that protection to finally remove is happening inside government right now, with no guarantee the answer actually lowers what you pay.

## Hook (first 30 seconds)

Open directly on the price comparison — the same car, dramatically different prices, framed through the episode's own title device (a car costing what a house deposit costs). No invented individual buyer (consistent with EP001's approach — don't fabricate a person we don't have sourced), but make the number itself concrete and visceral rather than abstract, the way EP001 converted its frozen-account figure into a felt scale.

## Ending

Land on the buyer's stake and a clear, specific thing to watch for (does the sticker price actually move when tariffs fall) rather than a tidy resolution — because the real story doesn't have one yet. This should feel like genuine, current-tense uncertainty, not a cop-out non-ending.

## Notes carried forward to Script Writing

- **Do not state the National Tariff Policy's headline target as a single figure** — the research brief flags two conflicting numbers (10.6%→6% vs. 20.19%→9.7%) that are still unreconciled. Either omit the specific target or explicitly note the range/discrepancy rather than picking one.
- **Do not name "PAMADA" as the cartelized association without resolving the motorcycle/car-dealer naming inconsistency** flagged in the research brief — describe the 2015 case by its facts (price-fixing, penalty amount, year) without asserting exactly which association, or resolve it first.
- **Phrase the AIDEP 2026-31 status in a way that ages gracefully** — this was unresolved as of 13 Aug 2026 sourcing; use dated, hedged language ("as of [date]...") rather than a flat present-tense claim that could be stale by air date.
- **The India comparison needs the fairness caveat the research brief flagged** — don't let the "7-8x more expensive" figure stand completely unqualified; the CCP report's note that India's own market is "moderately concentrated" (Maruti Suzuki >40%) is a start, but this section should stay measured rather than implying Pakistan's prices are purely a policy failure with no structural context.
