# Standing Knowledge — Textbook Board System

Contributed by: EP003 ("The Textbook Mafia: Why School Books Get Reprinted Every Year")

## Summary

Since the 1959 Sharif Commission (Commission on National Education), Pakistani provinces have required school textbooks to come through officially-created "textbook boards" — Punjab Curriculum and Textbook Board (PCTB, being restructured into PECTAA as of 2026-27), Sindh Textbook Board, KP Textbook Board, Balochistan Textbook Board. Originally a quality-control measure, it became a near-monopoly with real financial and political leverage over private publishers.

## Key mechanics

- Private publishers can produce textbooks but need a No Objection Certificate (NOC) from the relevant provincial board
- Under the 2021 Single National Curriculum (Punjab implementation): NOC cost Rs 140,000 per book (Rs 45,000 to the Muttahida Ulema Board for religious-content review, Rs 80,000 to external review committees, Rs 15,000 to PCTB itself), plus a 7.5% royalty per printed copy
- Sindh rejected the SNC entirely, preserving publisher autonomy; KP adopted a lighter version without the religious-review layer
- Boards have used their power directly: a 2020 incident saw ~100 textbooks banned and ~10,000 books from 31 publishers (including Oxford and Cambridge) seized; the official who ordered it was later removed, reportedly under publisher pressure

## Key figures

- ~40.75 million total students enrolled nationally (all levels, 2021-23); 81% of children aged 6-16 in government schools
- 2026-27 Punjab reform: 29 textbooks sanctioned for replacement; publishers claim ~Rs 2 billion in losses; PECTAA replaces PCTB, QAED, and the Punjab Examination Commission
