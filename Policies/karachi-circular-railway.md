# Standing Knowledge — Karachi Circular Railway (KCR)

Contributed by: EP005 ("The Karachi Circular Railway: The Train That's Been 'Coming Soon' for 30 Years")

## Summary

KCR was a 43.24km commuter rail loop around Karachi, running since 1964, carrying up to 6 million passengers a year at its peak. Losses and deteriorating maintenance led to full suspension of service by **December 1999**. Since then, it has been the subject of at least **14 distinct, named, publicly-announced revival attempts** (2003-2026) across four different governments and three different would-be financiers (JICA → CPEC/China → ADB) — none of which has produced a completed revival. The only tangible outcome in three decades is a single **14km partial segment** (City Station–Orangi Town), reopened in November 2020.

## The recurring stall mechanism (reusable for any Karachi-infrastructure or CPEC-adjacent episode)

1. **Land encroachment**: Thousands of informal residential/commercial structures sit on the KCR right-of-way (JICA's own 2013 survey counted 4,030 residential + 629 commercial structures). Clearing them is politically costly and has required direct Supreme Court intervention (Nov 2018 order, Justice Gulzar Ahmed).
2. **Federal vs. Sindh jurisdictional dispute**: Pakistan Railways (federal) owns the land; Karachi/Sindh gets the benefit. Recurring standoff over who pays for/transfers the land — Sindh wants a nominal Re. 1/sq yard transfer, the federal government wants standard acquisition payment. Recurred as recently as 2024.
3. **Financier-government chicken-and-egg**: JICA (2013-2016) wouldn't finance until encroachments were cleared; Sindh wouldn't clear encroachments until financing was locked in. This exact standoff caused JICA's 2016 withdrawal. The same underlying pattern — reluctance to move first — has resurfaced with every subsequent financier.
4. **Project cost inflation between approval stages**: CDWP recommended the project at Rs27.6 billion (early 2017); ECNEC approved the PC-1 at $1.97 billion (~Rs207bn) months later — a ~7x jump within the same year. The cost was revised at least 4 times by 2022. Useful general illustration of how Pakistani megaproject costs balloon between planning and formal approval.

## Key figures

- Peak historical ridership: ~6 million/year (104 daily trains)
- Full route: 43.24km, 24 stations (JICA's authoritative 2016-17 survey)
- Only currently-built segment: 14km, City Station–Orangi Town (since Nov 2020)
- Cumulative financing history: JICA (offered, withdrew 2016) → CPEC/China (formally included Dec 2016, "failed to gain priority status" per Dawn's Feb 2026 reporting — a decade with no real construction) → ADB ($1 billion committed May 2026, but only $10 million released so far, for preparatory/planning work only)
