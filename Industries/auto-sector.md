# Standing Knowledge — Automotive Industry

Contributed by: EP002 ("The Auto Tax: Why a Small Car Costs a House Deposit")

## Summary

Pakistan's car market has historically been dominated by three assemblers — "the Big Three": **Pak Suzuki**, **Indus Motor Company (Toyota)**, and **Honda Atlas Cars** — combined **≈79-80% of the market as of FY2025** (source: Competition Commission of Pakistan's own Feb 2026 report — resolved a prior contradiction between secondary sources). Newer entrants have been eroding that dominance since roughly FY2019/2020: Kia Lucky Motors (founded 2017, 6% share), Hyundai Nishat Motors (founded 2017, 5% share), Sazgar Engineering (7%), plus Ghandhara Automobiles, MG, and Changan. Market concentration (HHI ≈2,765) is still, by the CCP's own threshold, "highly concentrated" — the narrative of increasing competition is real but the market hasn't crossed into genuinely competitive territory yet.

## Key figures

- Market share (FY2025, CCP): Suzuki 45%, Toyota 22-23%, Honda 12%, Sazgar 7%, Kia 6%, Hyundai 5%, Ghandhara 1%, Others 1%
- Deletion Program (1987-2004) indigenization achieved: 56-70% in cars, 81-88% in motorcycles, but stayed limited to lower-value components
- Historical CBU import tariffs under the Tariff-Based System (2006-16): 50% to over 100% depending on engine size
- CCP enforcement history in this sector: 2013 show-cause against Indus Motor Company (Toyota) for abuse of dominant position; 2015 cartelization finding against an industry association (PKR 140M penalty) — **naming of that association has an internal inconsistency in the source report, flagged, not yet resolved**
- Suzuki Alto price: ~PKR 2.99-3.3 million (Pakistan, 2025-26) vs ~Rs 3.25-5.12 lakh (India, 2025-26) — commonly cited ~7-8x gap; India's own market for comparison is "moderately concentrated" with Maruti Suzuki over 40% (CCP report) — still needs a fuller fairness caveat before using the raw ratio uncritically
