## Newsletter summary

**Subject: The day Pakistan froze its own citizens' money**

In May 1998, Pakistan tested nuclear weapons — and on that same day, the government froze up to $11 billion held in ordinary Pakistanis' foreign currency bank accounts.

This week's video traces how that happened: a fragile IMF program that had been improving through early 1998, sanctions that hit within days of the tests, a bank run the government moved to stop by locking depositors out of their own money — money that existed specifically because a 1992 law had promised it would never be questioned.

By November 1998, the country's own reserves had fallen to roughly $400 million — a fraction of what had just been frozen — putting Pakistan weeks from formal default before a January 1999 IMF rescue and debt rescheduling pulled it back.

It's a story with a direct line to the present: it's a real part of why many older Pakistanis still keep dollar savings outside the formal banking system.
