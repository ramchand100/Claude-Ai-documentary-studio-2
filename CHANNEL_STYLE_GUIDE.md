# Channel Style Guide

This file is the single source of truth for voice and tone. Every script, title, and description must follow it. The Script Writing, Title/Thumbnail, and Content Repurposing skills all read this file.

## Audience

Ordinary Pakistani viewers — not economists, not policy wonks. Someone who has heard a term on the news (inflation, circular debt, IMF program, privatization) but couldn't explain it to a friend. Assume intelligence, not prior knowledge.

## Voice

- Explaining to a friend over chai, not lecturing from a podium.
- Conversational Urdu + simple English, mixed the way Pakistanis actually speak — not formal Urdu, not academic English.
- First person plural where natural ("humein," "we'll see") — the narrator is with the viewer figuring this out, not above them.
- Confident but not preachy. State facts; let the viewer draw conclusions on charged topics.

## Tone

- Curious, not cynical. Even on failure stories (a company collapse, a failed policy), the tone investigates "why" rather than mocking.
- Serious topics get respect, not sensationalism. No fear-mongering headlines that the video doesn't deliver on.
- Dry humor is fine when it fits; forced jokes are not.

## Storytelling rules

- Open with a hook, not a definition. Never start with "Pakistan's economy is..." — start with a person, a number, a moment, a question.
- Every episode needs a spine: a question the video is answering, or a change the video is tracking. State it early.
- Use one concrete example (a person, a company, a household) to carry an abstract concept (inflation, GDP, monetary policy).
- Build curiosity with open loops — raise a question, delay the answer, deliver it.
- End with why it matters to the viewer's own life, not just a summary of facts covered.

## Language rules

- Follow `GLOSSARY.md` for consistent bilingual terminology — don't re-translate the same term differently across episodes.
- Define jargon in one plain sentence the first time it's used. After that, use it freely.
- Use analogies drawn from everyday Pakistani life (household budgets, mandi prices, bijli bill, rent) to explain economic and policy concepts.
- Numbers: round for spoken delivery ("roughly 2.5 crore," not "24,873,000"), but keep the precise figure in the fact-check table and description.
- Short sentences for narration. Long sentences read fine; they don't speak well.

## Things to avoid

- Academic tone, textbook definitions, or reading a Wikipedia summary aloud.
- Political endorsement or attacking individuals — critique policies, systems, and decisions, not people's character or faith.
- Unverified claims stated as fact — see `SENSITIVITY_CHECKLIST.md` and the Fact-Checking skill.
- Fixed structure for every episode — a company story, a policy explainer, and a historical piece should not sound like the same template with different words swapped in (see the Documentary Structure skill).
- Clickbait titles the video doesn't deliver on — curiosity is earned, not tricked.
- Treating every episode's length the same. Let the topic decide: a focused explainer can be 8–10 minutes; a layered investigation can run 20+. Padding a short topic or rushing a deep one both hurt retention.

## Ownership

The AI drafts research, structure, script, and visual plans. You make the final calls on:
- which stories get made
- the angle and opinion (where the video takes a stance)
- final wording and edits
- what gets cut
