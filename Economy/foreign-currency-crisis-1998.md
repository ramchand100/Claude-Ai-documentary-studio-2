# Standing Knowledge — 1998 Foreign Currency Crisis

Contributed by: EP001 ("1998: The Year Pakistan Went Broke on Purpose")

## Summary

Following Pakistan's May 1998 nuclear tests, international sanctions and capital flight pushed foreign exchange reserves toward collapse. The Nawaz Sharif government froze up to $11 billion in onshore foreign currency accounts to stop a bank run. Reserves still fell to roughly $400 million by late November 1998. Resolved via a January 1999 IMF program and Paris/London Club debt rescheduling.

## Key figures

- Freeze date: **28 May 1998** — the same day as Pakistan's second round of nuclear tests, via the Foreign Exchange Temporary Restriction Ordinance (FETRO 1998)
- Frozen deposits: **$11.16 billion** as of 28 May 1998 (contemporaneous DAWN Wire Service figure; rounds to "up to $11 billion" in the earlier Gulf News/Arab News retrospective reporting)
- Legal basis for pre-freeze deposits: Protection of Economic Reforms Act, 1992 — gave foreign currency account holders "no questions asked" tax immunity, which is why deposits had built up
- Partial revival: 1 July 1998 — accounts "revived" for new deposits only; pre-28-May balances stayed restricted (single-sourced, treat as reported-not-certain)
- Reserves low point: ~$400 million (late Nov 1998)
- Official PKR/USD rate 1998: 46.0; parallel market rate: 54.2 (parallel rate flagged Medium confidence — see EP001 fact-check report)
- IMF disbursement (14 Jan 1999): ~$575 million (SDR 409.48 million)
